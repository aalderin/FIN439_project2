[0:25 - 1:25]
To start with the architecture: our pipeline flows from raw price data, into our financial math engine, and finally into an interactive Streamlit app. Here in prices.csv, we have 60 monthly adjusted-close prices for our four ETF sleeves: SGOV for cash, VGIT for Treasuries, VCIT for corporate credit, and VTI for broad equities, covering late 2021 through late 2026. In portfolio.py, the function estimate_from_csv processes that raw data: it converts monthly prices into monthly returns; it annualizes average returns by multiplying by 12; it annualizes volatility by multiplying standard deviation by the square root of 12; and it constructs our full 4-by-4 covariance matrix using pairwise correlations and individual volatilities. Everything is written in standard-library Python—no black-box optimization packages—so the entire math pipeline is completely transparent.

[1:25 - 2:30]
When we run python portfolio.py directly in the terminal, the main function evaluates our baseline benchmarks: Equal Weight gives only 5.20% expected return—failing our 6.5% mandate hurdle; a traditional 40/60 benchmark passes the return hurdle, but has 0% in liquid reserves and carries high volatility at 10.46%. To find a portfolio that actually beats these baselines, we look at the function lowest_risk_mix in portfolio.py. Instead of using an unconstrained continuous solver that gives fractional decimals, our code performs a discrete grid search in 5 percentage point steps across all four assets. For every combination that sums to 100%: it checks if expected return is at least 6.5%; it checks if reserve-eligible assets—summing SGOV and VGIT—are at least 3%; and for qualifying mixes, it computes portfolio variance w-transpose Sigma w, takes the square root for volatility, and saves the mix with the absolute lowest volatility.

[2:30 - 3:30]
Now let's launch the app with streamlit run app.py. When we upload prices.csv in the sidebar, the app runs lowest_risk_mix across our 60-month dataset. Looking at the screen under 'Lowest-risk mix': our optimal allocation is 65% SGOV and 35% VTI. Expected return is 6.79%, clearing our 6.5% target; volatility is just 5.62%—cutting risk nearly in half compared to the 60/40 benchmark; and reserve-eligible weight is 65.0%, well above the 3% minimum. Intermediate bonds get 0% because SGOV provides a 3.8% cash yield with almost zero volatility, dominating fixed income.

[4:25 - 4:45]
A primary limitation of this system is that historical estimates are not forward forecasts. If cash yields compress below 2.5%, or forward equity returns drop below 11%, the 65/35 mix can no longer meet our return objective without taking on more risk.
